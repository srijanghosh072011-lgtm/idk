02 How Paradise Dies

Nobody chooses this. It happens in five quiet steps.

/ 01

It starts with one bottle

A picnic, a festival, a bin that was already full. One piece of litter signals that this place is already lost — and gives the next person permission.

/ 02

The tide does the rest

Currents collect what we leave behind and redistribute it along the entire coastline. Your beach inherits the carelessness of every beach upstream.

/ 03

Plastic never leaves — it shrinks

Sun and salt grind bottles into microplastics that sink into the sand itself. Past this point, no cleanup crew on Earth can get it all back out.

/ 04

The reef starves, the fish vanish

Debris smothers coral and fills the stomachs of the fish that feed on it. The water turns from turquoise to the colour of what we put in it.

/ 05

Nobody photographs it anymore

Tourism leaves, the local economy follows, and the beach falls off the map. What was once paradise becomes a place people apologise for.

03 Turning The Tide

The decline took decades. The fix starts this weekend.

Beaches recover astonishingly fast when people show up. Here's where the tide actually turns — four moves, in order of impact.

Show up to a cleanup

Two hours, one morning, measurable results. A 30-person crew clears roughly a tonne of debris from a single stretch of sand — and the data we log drives policy.

Find one near you

Refuse single-use

The cheapest cleanup is the bottle that never exists. One reusable bottle replaces an average of 156 plastic ones a year — multiply that by a coastline of visitors.

Take the pledge

Back the policy

Bag bans, deposit schemes, and producer-responsibility laws cut coastal litter by up to 40% where they pass. Your signature and your vote scale further than your hands.

See active campaigns

Teach one person

Every volunteer we survey traces it back to a single conversation. Bring a friend to one cleanup and you've roughly doubled your lifetime impact on the shoreline.
